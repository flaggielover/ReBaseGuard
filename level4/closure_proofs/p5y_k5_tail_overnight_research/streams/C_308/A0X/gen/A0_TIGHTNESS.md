# A0 tightness note (C2b): how far a block-uniform Ā(E) exceeds sup_{e∈E} Λ(e)

Scope: non-target blocks E = [e0, e0 + W], with e0 ∈ {1/2, 1, 3} and W ∈ {1/40, 1/20, 1/10}, all outside the
quarantine band. Nothing here is evaluated at, or compared with, any tail cell.

**Theorem M.** An accepted review of Theorem M notes that certified upper bounds on Λ at |e| ≤ 1 logically transfer
to larger |e|. That implication exists and was not used anywhere in this stream: no bound here is transferred to,
stated for, or compared at any other drift.

## 1. Decomposition, with a floor that is a theorem

Let E ⊂ ℝ be a drift set and define the robust (adversarial, time-varying drift) value function

    W*_E = 1 + sup_{e∈E} K_e W*_E,       Λ*(E) := W*_E(a).

**Lemma (common-supersolution floor).** If w ≥ 0 and w ≥ 1 + K_e w on R for every e ∈ E, then w ≥ W*_E. Hence
every block-uniform supersolution certificate satisfies

    Ā(E) = w(a)  ≥  Λ*(E)  ≥  sup_{e∈E} Λ(e).

*Proof.* Let W_0 = 0 and W_{n+1} = 1 + sup_e K_e W_n. If W_n ≤ w, then by positivity of K_e,
W_{n+1} ≤ 1 + sup_e K_e w ≤ w. The sequence W_n increases to W*_E. For the second inequality: for each e ∈ E,
W*_E ≥ 1 + K_e W*_E, so W*_E is a K_e-supersolution and therefore W*_E ≥ V_e. ∎

So the excess splits into two parts:

    Ā(E) − sup Λ = [Λ*(E) − sup Λ]  +  [Ā(E) − Λ*(E)]
                   intrinsic floor       certification overhead
                   (any common-w method)  (this strategy: block proposal, mesh h, α-ladder, interpolation error)

The same floor applies to I1's Ā and to I2's Ā, since both are common supersolutions over the block. It does
**not** apply to a τ/D_lo route, whose block-uniformity costs sup τ_e / inf D_e instead. That is a different
trade-off and is not measured here.

## 3. Findings

1. **The intrinsic floor is negligible on these blocks.** Λ*(E) − sup Λ is at most 0.003% (e0 = 1/2), about 1e-8
   relative (e0 = 1) and 0 (e0 = 3). The adversary picks a larger drift only at a handful of nodes; for
   [1/2, 21/40] that is 128 of 13121. A common supersolution with atom value essentially sup Λ exists.
2. **The block excess is overhead of this strategy, and it grows with width.** It is 4.9 → 9.8 → 20.0% at
   e0 = 1/2 and 0.95 → 1.6 → 2.5% at e0 = 1, as W goes 1/40 → 1/20 → 1/10. That is roughly linear in W, and
   larger where Λ(e) falls steeply: Λ drops about 31% over [1/2, 3/5] and about 14% over [1, 11/10]. At e0 = 3 the
   excess is flat at the pointwise level (α-ladder resolution).
3. **Diagnosis.** The declared block proposal is g = nodal max of the two end-point value functions, which is ≈ V_{e_lo}.
   It is not a common supersolution at states where a larger drift delays the alarm. The deficit is then paid
   through β (1.8 → 7.5 at e0 = 1/2) or α (1.0095 → 1.025 at e0 = 1), and both lift w(a).
4. **Remedy: DEFERRED, not run, would be amendment r1.** Use the robust value function W*_E (c2b_float.robust) as
   the block proposal. By finding 1 its atom value is ≈ sup Λ, so the block overhead should fall towards the
   pointwise level. This is untested. The strategy as fixed in STRATEGY.md is what was validated.
5. **The A0 floor is not the binding term here.** Any other common-supersolution producer (I1 and I2 Ā included)
   also faces the §1 floor. On these non-target blocks that floor is tiny, so a large Ā excess from such a producer
   would be method overhead, not an intrinsic limit. No such producer was evaluated here.
