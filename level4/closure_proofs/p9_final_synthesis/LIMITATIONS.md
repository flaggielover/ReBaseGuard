# P9 limitations

## 1. P9's own standing

**P9 has no frozen definition.** The repository contains zero statements
defining a Level-4 Priority 9 (`P9_DEFINITION_AUDIT.md` §2). Every scope item is
`PROMPT_DERIVED`. An adjudicator who does not accept that a campaign prompt can
constitute a priority should read this namespace as a *synthesis audit of
P1–P8*, not as a Level-4 closure step. Nothing here depends on the stronger
reading.

**P8 is `FAIL`.** The authoritative adjudication landed during this campaign.
P9 held no P8 premise, so no P9 conclusion changed — but it also means P9 adds
**nothing** about behaviour outside the two frozen Gaussian specialisations.

**P9's theorem `P9-T1` is elementary.** It is a minimum over a DAG. P9 does not
present it as a deep result; its value is the `verifies`/`premise` distinction
and the audit it licenses. Its soundness reading is a *policy* with a rationale,
not a proved metatheorem.

## 5. Methodological limitations of this campaign

* **P9's closure gates were written after its reproductions ran**
  (`EXPERIMENT_PROTOCOL.md` §0). They are therefore restricted to
  verifiable-by-inspection artifact properties, not numerical thresholds P9
  could have tuned. A4–A6 had **no** pre-set pass/fail threshold and are
  reported descriptively.
* The claim ledger is a **reading** of the repository. It was assembled by one
  agent from the adjudication records; a different reader could reasonably
  assign a different tier to a borderline claim. The ledger records a `source`
  for every row precisely so that disagreement can be localised.
* P9's reproduction implementation is independent of P1–P8 but was written by
  the same agent that wrote this synthesis. It is not a third-party replication.
